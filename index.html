edge stack

How money is actually evaluated.

The page needs to make advanced trading feel learnable. A setup is not valuable because it looks clean. It matters only if the numbers, execution, and risk path survive testing.

01 / thesis

Why should price move?

Liquidity, inventory pressure, volatility expansion, session flow, or a mispriced reaction.

if reason == vague: skip
02 / feature

What can be measured?

Sweep depth, displacement size, time of day, range location, FVG width, and close quality.

feature = clean + repeatable
03 / expectancy

Does it pay after losses?

Win rate alone means nothing. Average win, average loss, fees, slippage, and missed fills decide edge.

EV = P(win)*W - P(loss)*L
04 / execution

Can you enter without killing it?

A perfect chart can turn negative when spread widens, liquidity thins, or entry is late.

net edge = gross edge - costs
05 / sizing

Can the account survive variance?

Risk of ruin is the real boss. Position size should respect losing streaks before upside.

survival > aggression
06 / review

What changes after feedback?

Journal tags show whether the model is broken or the trader is breaking the model.

improve only what is measured

Expected value

Profit starts with positive expectancy, not confidence.

A setup can lose more often than it wins and still be profitable if average wins are large enough. It can also win often and still die if losses are larger or costs are ignored.

EV = winRate * avgWinR - lossRate * avgLossR

complex concepts made usable

Quant language without the ego.

The point is not to sound smart. The point is to turn hard ideas into questions a trader can answer before entering a trade.

Microstructure

Who needs liquidity right now, and who is forced to accept bad price?

Liquidity sweep

A move through obvious highs or lows that can trap late breakout traders.

FVG / imbalance

A fast price delivery zone that may attract a later return or continuation.

Regime filter

The setup behaves differently in trend, range, high-volatility, and news conditions.

Slippage

The hidden cost between the price you planned and the price you actually get.

R-multiple

A normalized way to compare trades by risk, not by emotional dollar size.

Overfitting

When a strategy memorizes old charts instead of learning behavior that repeats.

Monte Carlo

Shuffle the same trades into many paths to see drawdown pain before it happens.

Kelly fraction

A sizing idea that shows why most traders should risk far less than their ego wants.

Journal drift

The gap between the model you wrote and the trades you actually took.
